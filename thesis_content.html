Cybersecurity in Embedded Systems

Thesis (2026) • Case study of Industrial protocols (Modbus & CAN-bus)

My research focuses on securing industrial environments. I utilize a self-developed visualization of the Purdue Model (PERA) to illustrate the importance of network segmentation in embedded systems.

Figure 1: Visualization of industrial network layers and protocol-specific interfaces.

Analysis: Chapter 2.2

In my work, I specifically focus on the following critical areas:

  • Level 1 (Basic Control): How CAN-bus and Modbus (RTU) are protected at the physical level.
  • Level 2 (Supervisory Control): Modbus (TCP) traffic between SCADA workstations and HMI devices and its monitoring.
  • Level 3.5 (DMZ): The importance of "air gap" thinking and Jump Servers in secure remote management.

Practical application

The thesis is not just theory, but provides recommendations on how firewalls (black brick walls in the picture) should be configured to prevent lateral movement between the IT network and the production network.

Example scenario: Social manipulation

Scenario: The attacker pretends to be a job seeker and sends a portfolio link to an engineer at the target company.

// Attack vector

1. The user clicks the "Upload CV" button.
2. The browser downloads and executes a hidden attack vector.
3. The attacker gains remote control (C2) over a device in the IT network.
4. The attacker aims to progress from level 4 of the Purdue model towards the production network (Level 2-3).

This highlights that humans are often the weakest link. Even if an industrial network is well segmented, a compromised maintenance machine can provide an attacker with turnkey access to critical infrastructure.
